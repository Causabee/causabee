Causabee in numbers

27 September to 13 October 2026

Causabee was built in seventeen days by one designer working with Claude Code. This page holds the numbers behind the articles about how that went. They are counted from the Git history and from the 27 working sessions, which were kept as text.

The seventeen days

Three days of a command-line spike on real mail, two days of going public, then steady daily use and polish.

From the first beta on, each piece of work ended with a signed build on every device. That was the test bench, not a goal: the demo holds only made-up matters, and real ones live in iCloud across three devices, so testing on real data needed a real build on each.

What a designer typed

None of the 1,344 messages was code. The middle one is six words long. One in eight carried a screenshot, 160 in all, and twelve carried a screen recording.

Claude answered with 12,788 tool calls, between five and fourteen for each message depending on the day; 1.8% of them returned an error. Critique outnumbered praise about two to one, 157 messages against 81, counted by wording.

Who decided what

A decision is a moment where the designer chose, rejected or redirected. They were written down hour by hour and sorted into three kinds: what the product does, how it looks and reads, and how it is built.

Design moved between Figma and code in both directions, but not equally. Claude wrote to the Figma file 592 times and read a design’s specification from it twice. From 4 October, changes were asked for in words and screenshots against the running app, and Figma was brought in line afterwards.

Measured against Apple’s guidelines

On 9 October the three apps were reviewed against Apple’s Human Interface Guidelines. The review found 50 things to change.

PlatformRating at the reviewWhy
iPhone4 of 5System navigation, sheets and text sizes were right; small touch targets and untitled bars were not.
Mac3 of 5Settings, context menus and drag-in were right; the menu bar covered a fraction of the app and there was no undo.
iPad2 of 5It adapted to its size, but had no keyboard shortcuts, no menu commands and no drag and drop.

A day later 16 of the 50 were fixed in whole or in part, among them every High finding on the Mac and the iPad. Fourteen were left alone on purpose. The apps have not been rated again since.

What reached the AI

Before any text goes to a model, Causabee replaces what identifies a person. This is the count of what was still readable in the 677 mails of the last three test runs from the first three days, when the disguise was new, and after a fix made on the day of the count.

So names and addresses did not get through, and two things did. Long numbers with no label in front of them were not seen as numbers that point to a person. And a link was sent as “[link]”, but where its address had been disguised first, the part after it stayed. Both were found by this count and fixed the same day; the same mails, run again, show none of either.

How this was counted: the sent text was searched for each pattern, and for every original the disguise had recorded. The fixed disguise was run over the already disguised texts, not the original mails. Still readable by design: numbers of six or seven digits with no label, because hiding those would hide amounts and years too. A first name standing alone can stay if the name-finder does not know it. A second, smaller set of labelled people and companies is still being checked. The fix went out on 14 October, in 0.5 Beta 69 for the Mac and build 67 for iPhone and iPad.

Where the numbers come from

  • Commits and lines of code are read from the public repository; the first three days from the private history’s own counts.
  • Messages and tool calls are counted from the 27 sessions, each session and each repeated message once.
  • Decisions are counted from three hourly protocols written from those sessions.
  • The ratings are from the review in the repository, under docs/hig-review.

Counted on 13 October 2026. The sessions themselves are not public, because they hold personal mail.
