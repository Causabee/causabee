# F009 · Causabee in German — Looks

## Screens and states

No screen changes shape: every screen is the one it is today, with German words in it. What German does change is length — its words are about a third longer — so these are the places to look at, each in its states (empty, one, many, long text, an error, without Pro), on all three devices:

| Where | What German presses on |
|---|---|
| The welcome's four pages | The promise's two lines, and one line under each picture |
| The first step | The title, the line under it, the three add buttons side by side (`ViewThatFits` already stacks them), the start credit's line |
| The overview | The week strip's weekday letters, "Letzte Änderungen" and "Anliegen" as tabs, a matter's card |
| A matter's page | The five tabs — the longest words in the app's smallest space |
| The Next card | "Als erledigt markieren", longer than "Done" |
| Plan and usage | The bars' words, the plans' names and what each holds |
| The mail card, the start credit's card | Two buttons and a line, in a card |
| Menus (`Say`) | Title Case in English; German capitalises nouns instead |
| Notifications | One line on a locked screen, cut by the system when long |
| Alerts and errors | The plain words of a failure |

A German line that does not fit is rewritten shorter, never squeezed or shrunk (AC-5, principle 10).

The rest of the app's words — about 700 — are written into the catalogue in these same rules, and the owner reads every one of them on their own device before the Build gate. They are not listed here one by one: the catalogue is the list, and the test of AC-1 is what says none is missing.

**Checked against the house rules:** short sentences, few words, no jargon; never mail-only — "Aus dem, was du hast" names every source, and mail is named only on the mail card, which is about mail alone.

## On each device

- **The same everywhere:** every German word comes from the two catalogues, so one thing has one German word on the iPhone, the iPad and the Mac (AC-4).
- **iPhone:** the tightest place, and where German will press hardest — the five tabs of a matter, the week strip, the add buttons.
- **iPad:** two columns; the sidebar's words are the overview's.
- **Mac:** the menu bar as well — `Say`'s 28 words, and the app's own menu (Über Causabee, Einstellungen …), which macOS writes itself for a German app.

## Undo, and what the user can take back

Nothing to undo: the language is the device's. A person who wants English back sets it back, for Causabee alone or for the whole device, and nothing in their matters changes either way (AC-8).

## Accessibility

- **VoiceOver** reads German too (AC-10): every label, hint and spoken line is in the catalogue, including the bars' spoken words (`ProMeter.spoken`) and the pictures' hidden descriptions.
- **Large type:** German's longer words with the largest size is the hardest case; every screen must still wrap, scroll and stack rather than cut.
- **Dark mode, targets, Reduce Motion:** untouched.

## Design system

No new part, colour or font. Every screen keeps the parts it has. One line is added to `docs/design-system.md`: **a word comes from the catalogue, never written twice** — the German side of C4 ("one thing, one word, on every device") and C5 (`Say`).

Not needed: no screen changes shape, and what German changes is the words themselves, which are in the tables above. What a picture could not show — whether a German line fits at the largest text size — is checked on the three devices at Build, and by the owner's own look.
