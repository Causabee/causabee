# The Bee — Manifesto

**The bee carries the mental load, so you don't have to.**
You live your life. She keeps your matters in order.

---

## 1. Everything counts. Not only mail.

What comes in, from anywhere, is the same to her:

- mail
- screenshots — WhatsApp, Signal, any chat
- photos — a letter on the table, a whiteboard, a receipt; from the library, or taken right then in the chat
- files and scans — PDFs, contracts, invoices
- your notes, typed or spoken
- what you say in the chat
- tasks and dates you add yourself

**Mail is one source. Never the centre.**

## 2. Matters, not messages.

You think in matters: the roof, Mum's care, the move.
You never file. You only say which mail is hers (a label) — screenshots, photos and files you hand her directly. She does the rest.

## 3. What she does for you

| She… | So you don't have to… |
|---|---|
| **Brings in** what comes in and **sorts** it into its matter | file anything |
| **Reads** it and pulls out tasks, dates, deadlines, people — a reply with what it answers: "Freitag passt" takes the time that was offered | copy anything out |
| **Understands** each matter: what it is about, where it stands | remember it |
| **Decides the next step** — one, yours, doable | think about what's next |
| **Keeps what is good to know** — true, asks nothing of you | look it up again |
| **Tells you what is new** — once, clearly | check everything again |
| **Adds** what comes in, marked NEW; **proposes** changes to what is yours | worry she changed something behind your back |
| **Keeps your Calendar and Reminders in step** with your matters, both ways | enter a date twice |
| **Looks things up** on the web when you tap — a legal period, what an insurer pays | research |
| **Answers** in the chat, from your matters only, and does what you ask | search |

She works by herself while **Auto** is on. Off, she waits for your tap.

## 4. Principles — check every change against these

1. **Every source counts the same.** Nothing is mail-only unless it truly is. (12 November: the chat's footer said "goes pseudonymised, like the mails"; tested by `ManifestoTests` "1 The chat's footer names no source alone".)
2. **New is said once.** New means it *reached the bee* after you last opened the matter — not the date written on it. A screenshot taken last week, shared today, is new today. Reworded is not new. Re-read is not new.
3. **One signal, one meaning.** The yellow dot and NEW say the same thing: something came you haven't looked at. Opening the matter — on any tab — takes them away. Nothing else does.
4. **Looked at is not handled.** Like a read mail: still there until you act — 👍, ×, done, Seen.
5. **She adds, she never changes.** Tasks and dates from what comes in are added, marked NEW — you keep them (👍) or put them away (×). What is already yours — done, a new day, its words — changes only with your tap. An edit you make in Calendar or Reminders counts as your tap. A date you set — in the chat, in its sheet, on its day — is yours from then on: what comes in later is only added to its sources, and when the other side moves it, their new date comes as a date of its own, marked NEW, for you to keep or put away. The same day said again never takes its hour away. Undo gives it back.
6. **Your word wins.** What you said or wrote beats what came in.
7. **One next step.** Yours, concrete, doable. Never something already done.
8. **Good to know is stable.** True, asks nothing, the same fact never comes back as new.
9. **Less, not more.** Every line must earn its place. When in doubt, leave it out. Noise is load.
10. **Clear words.** Short sentences. Few words. No jargon, no ids.
11. **Private.** No message is ever sent in your name. Content leaves the device only with names disguised — by itself only with Auto on, otherwise at your tap. What Causabee says about privacy — in the app, on the website, in the notes of a release — is exactly what it does: where full texts go, who reads what comes in, which AI. Never more than is true (10 November: the website said full texts are "never synced", a line before saying they go to iCloud; tested by `SaidAsItIsTests`). A film or a picture of the app shows the app as it is today, as it behaves: a suggestion is taken or turned down, not called a task; nothing is shown before it happens (10 November: the website's film showed October's app, called a suggestion a task, and showed the chat's question before it was asked; tested by `FilmAnewTests` F002 AC-1 and AC-3).
12. **Everywhere the same.** iPhone, iPad, Mac: one matter, one state. A brief is written once, for every device: the one that wrote it says which record it is for, and the others take it as it is — minutes or days later. It is written again only when you change something in the matter or something new comes in; never because another device opens.
13. **She writes as you do.** A mail or a message she drafts sounds like you, to that person: the greeting, the sign-off, du or Sie, as you and they write to each other. Never more formal than they are. A reply in a running exchange is a reply, not a letter. Your words in the chat set its length — "Freitag passt" is two lines. Textbook form only for a first message to an office, when nothing else is known. A person by first name, or by title and surname — never both; a firm without a name. The language of the exchange. Your shorthand made clear to the reader ("my mother, Helga Voss"). A change you ask of a draft — shorter, warmer, signed "Mara" — changes it.
14. **A date says when and how long.** "14:30–15:30 · 1 h" — how long only when it is known, never Calendar's guessed hour. One without a time says "No time yet" — never a bare day that hides it. You set how long as a duration — "3 h" — not as an end.
15. **Waiting is not doing.** What someone else has to do never looks like yours: no box to tick, who you wait for first, greyed, a dashed line round it instead of a card.
16. **Latest is what happened.** Latest updates puts a matter on top only when something happened there: something came in, or you added, ticked or wrote something. Her rewording — a fact said again, a task written down again — is not news.
17. **Linked means in step.** A date linked to a Calendar event shows what the event says; what it lacks — a time, an end, a place — is taken from the event. A time gone here is never sent there to take Calendar's away. What you change here goes there with its end. When Calendar will not take it — an invitation, a work calendar, a server that puts it back, even later — your word stays here, and the card says what Calendar holds. An invitation is never written to.
18. **What you have and whether you use it are two things.** Pro and top-ups are bought; "Use Pro" decides whether they are used now. Turning it off takes nothing away — the plan and its bars stay, greyed, "not in use". Running out does not turn it off: it waits until the month starts anew or a top-up comes, and nothing is sent meanwhile. Whatever you have is always shown, even before it is counted (12 November: the switch was called "Pro on", the Mac said "Free" beside it, and a used-up month turned it off; tested by `PlanWordsTests` "F007 AC-4" and "F007 AC-7"; and the iPhone said "Causabee Pro" five days after Pro ended there — what this device's App Store saw end is not given back by iCloud's copy, tested by `PlanHeldTests`).
19. **Simple to start, more as you grow.** The first start asks for nothing before it is needed: a few calm pages, then your own first matter — typed or added. Mail, iCloud, Pro, your name and notifications come when they help, never at the door; the demo is there for whoever is stuck, never first (12 November: the first start was a checklist of parts, mail set up before anything else, and notifications asked at the first matter; tested by `WelcomeTests` "F008 AC-7" and "F008 AC-13").

## 5. The chat

The chat is you talking to her about your matters.
- She answers from what is in your matters — the words themselves, not only her summaries — and says when it isn't there only after looking in them.
- A quick question is answered from what it needs: the brief, what is open, the dates to come, what came in lately. A question that needs the whole matter gets all of it, and she says something isn't there only after looking in the whole matter — never from the part she was shown (11 November: every question sent the whole matter, the first one in a conversation about $0.05; tested by `ChatDepthTests` "A light answer that lacks something is asked again deep", "An element only the whole matter holds sends the question deep", and the pressure test in `docs/features/005-the-chat-in-two-depths/tests/`). A conversation lasts as long as the cache, 30 minutes: a question after a pause is a first question again, whatever the matter's chat said before (12 November: the chat is kept from day to day, and every question in an old thread went deep at full price; tested by "A follow-up is one within the cache's 30 minutes").
- What she offers to do is a card: you take it, or not.
- **You control everything from the chat.** Every element of a matter — what came in, tasks, what is good to know, dates, people, notes, details, links, files, decisions, the matter itself — she sees, can answer about, and can change for you: add it, put it away, open it again, change one thing of it (a place, a phone number, a name, a value), move it to another matter, close the matter. Each card can be undone. A card that cannot be done says why; it never claims what did not happen.
- What you take lands where you'd look for it: a note in Notes, a task in Plan, a date in the calendar.
- What she writes for you to send, she writes in your voice (principle 13).
- What you tell her to change, changes as you said it — the day, the time, how long — and stays. Nothing she reads later moves it. "Date changed" shows the date as it is now; if anything ever changed it back, the receipt says so, with Set again — never a tick on what is not so. A card acts on exactly the item it names, and on nothing else: never on another item that only shares its number in the store. Every button on a card works, every time: Set again, Undo and taking a card never close the app — a card that cannot do it says why (10 November: "Change date" on the test drive changed an unrelated task, and Set again closed the app; tested by `ChatControlTests` "An id asked for as another kind is none", "Fetched by another kind's id: none", and `SetAgainUITests`).
- What you tell her shapes her understanding at once and wins over what came in. It is your own word, so it is never shown back to you as NEW.

## 6. How we know she works

Each job is tested against its promise:

- **Sort** — every item ends up in exactly one matter, or in none. Sample set with known answers.
- **Read** — a task only when someone must act; a date only when it is in the source. Sample set.
- **Understand / next step / good to know** — sample matters with expected briefs.
- **New** — replay days of things coming in, of every kind: what says NEW each morning, and only that.
- **Screens** — automated tests on iPhone, iPad and Mac for every signal: dot, NEW, card, receipt.

- **Dates** — `DateMatrixTests`: every way a date is set (chat, sheet, day tile, Calendar) × everything after it (what comes in of every kind, the sync's passes, a server putting it back early or late, an invitation, a failed write, undo), and what the card and the receipt then say; times as people write them, in German and English.
- **Chat control** — `ChatControlTests`: every element put away and shown again, each field changed and set back, each moved and back, every element seen by an id.
- **Offered, then settled** — a time offered in one message and settled in the reply reaches the date; the chat finds it in the words.
- **Write** — `matter-spike pressure`: 24 situations of nine kinds of people, sent as the chat sends them, recorded whole and drawn on the screen; the report is `docs/message-pressure-test.md`. Its disguise findings hold in `DisguiseFindingsTests`.
- **A cheaper model** — a job moves to a cheaper model only after it does the job as well as the model before, on the demo's invented matters: the same matter for every item, at least 95% of the tasks and dates, nothing invented; the brief judged blind by the owner. `matter-spike jobs`, its answers kept with the feature; the table of jobs and models is one, the same in the app and on the server (`AIJobTests`).
- **One brief for every device** — `BriefHoldsTests`: a brief written elsewhere an hour ago, for the record as it stands, is not written again; a new item, a task ticked or what you say of the matter has it written again; looking at it does not; a brief from a build before is taken as before. In the demo, `AutoTests.testADemoFilledAnewKeepsItsSteps`: filled anew on a new day, its steps hold on every device.

If a change breaks a principle, it is wrong — however good it looks.

Every rule found while using her goes in here, the day it is found.

## 7. Known gaps — where the code does not yet keep a promise

Listed so nobody pretends. Each one closes with a test.

- **Section 3, Reads:** files attached to mail are read by themselves on iPhone and iPad after each mail check (logos and signatures skipped). The Mac does not do it on its own yet; a file dropped into a matter's folder is kept until you tap Read.
- **Principle 12:** two devices rewriting at once can, rarely, bring back a NEW you already saw. Planned fix, no schema change: each device keeps its own last-seen time and never writes an older one back; the brief records which device wrote it.
- **Principle 12, one brief:** when something new reaches two open devices at the same moment, each can still write the brief once, before the other's has arrived; the later one stays. And a brief written before 11 November does not yet say which record it is for: until it is written once more, a device takes it over only when it is under ten minutes old.
- **Principle 13:** she is given how the newest messages open and close, and the rules to write as you do; the pressure test shows she mostly keeps them, not always (a greeting now and then). Nothing checks each draft after it is written.
- **Chat, the words themselves:** she is given the words of the ten newest items of a matter; an older one only by its summary.
- **Dates across devices:** a device on an older build can still undo a date with its old rules, and iCloud carries that to the others. Every device on the same build.
- **Dates restated:** when you moved a date and a later item still names the old one, that old date comes as a NEW date of its own — put it away with ×.
- **Times in words:** "halb drei", "Viertel nach zwei" are not read yet; the bee's answer carries them.
- **Chat control:** a detail's value is kept from the bee on purpose — she knows a detail is there, not what it says. Undo of a card lasts while the app is open. Moving something to a matter the chat does not show works by that matter's name.
